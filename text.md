### 7.3. ~~Potential applications~~ (Потенциальные применения) of ~~BSG bioactive fractions~~ (биоактивных фракций BSG) in ~~pet and animal food~~ (корме для домашних и сельскохозяйственных животных)

Unrefined BSG which is largely used as animal feed, mainly for cattle has been reported not to be a suitable protein source therefore assessment of protein quality must be undertaken for the BSG-derived protein concentrates and hydrolysates rather than for the untreated BSG. The low of solubility of BSG proteins presents a barrier to their extensive use in food. Additionally, intact BSG use in food remains limited due to its unpleasant flavour and aroma, attributed to compounds like 2-butyl-1-octanol, 3-methyl-butanal, 2-heptane, butanal, benzene, and 2,3-butanedione, which contribute to its characteristic odour (Spinelli et al., 2016). BBF holds significant potential for food applications due to their key techno-functional properties, and bioactivities. As shown in Tables 5 and 6, hydrolysis of BSG has the advantage of increasing protein yield, releasing and increasing the availability of amino acids, especially hydrophobic amino acids (Val, Phe, Ala, Leu, Ile). With this potential, BBF can be used as pet and farm animal feed ingredients and just like by-products such as rice bran, soy flakes and isolates and oats fibre.

The nutritional requirements of cats and dogs, as recommended by FEDIAF (Tables 8 and 9), as well as those for cattle by the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (NASEM), can be met with BBF. Although classified as carnivores, cats and dogs have specific nutrient requirements, with cats having more specialized needs, particularly high protein and fat, than dogs. Significant levels of all the EAA – Arg, His, Ile, Leu, Lys, Met, Phe, Thr, Trp, and Val – are present in BSH (Tables 5). Additionally, BSH has Arginine, an EAA required for the growth, detoxification and excretion of ammonia as urea. The sensitivity of mammals to dietary arginine deficiency is cats > dogs > rats. In cats, insufficient dietary arginine causes reduced food intake, hyperammonemia (within 1–3 hours post-feeding), vomiting, neurological symptoms, ataxia, tetanic spasms, and potentially death due to impaired ureagenesis (Morris & Rogers, 1978). In dogs, arginine deficiency results in decreased food intake, hyperammonemia, severe emesis, frothing at the mouth, muscle tremors, and cataracts in puppies. Dietary supplementation of pet food with arginine or citrulline is essential for preventing these conditions. BBF can equally supply the mineral requirements set by FEDIAF (1984) for cats and dogs and NASEM (2016) for cattle (Table 10).

Dietary fats in pet foods are classified as facilitative or functional, rather than good or bad, as in humans, due to dogs’ and cats’ greater resistance to the cholesterol-raising effects of certain fats. Functional fats like linoleic acid (LA) support healthy skin and coat, promote gastrointestinal and renal health, enhance reproduction, control
inflammation, and aid neurological development, whereas Docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) plays a vital role in retinal function, converting light energy into neurological signals critical for vision. Studies have found that puppies consuming diet supplemented with DHA consistently had improvements in sensitivity to light, highlighting
DHA’s essential role in vision and neural development. Saturated fats and trans fats enhance palatability, improve
texture, provide energy, and aid in fat-soluble vitamin absorption. Dogs and cats readily use saturated fats through the
β-oxidation process to produce ATP to fuel other metabolic processes; however, these fats should not be given in excessive amounts as they predispose pets to risks of obesity and related health issues. The lipid and fatty acids in BSG can be used in pet food formulations to meet the fat and fatty acid requirements outlined by FEDIAF (Tables 8 and 9) for cats and dogs, offering a nutritional, healthy, and palatable option for both pet and farm animal diets.

With the increasing number of pet foods available on the market, palatability, which is defined as the physical and chemical properties of the diet, which are linked with the promotion or suppression of feeding behaviour during the pre-absorptive period It is a major criterion used to measure product performance . Lysine and other amino acids such as tyrosine, arginine, and tryptophan have been widely explored as flavour enhancers in human and pet foods. In U.S. Patent No. 4267,195, Boudreau & White (1981) reported increased palatability of dry dog food coated with L-lysine. Niceron et al. (2014) have also noted an increased palatability of dry cat food coated with a mixture of amino acids in E.P Patent No. 2731,449 B The rich amino acid profile of BSH while meeting the requirements in pets may offer positive palatability outcomes in pet food and farm animal feed. Amino acids like L-proline, L-cysteine, L-lysine, L-histidine, and L-alanine, which are identified as ‘sweet’ and preferred by cats, are present in significant amounts in BSH.

Chemical changes in pet food are largely driven by the pro-oxidant effects of oxygen and light, leading to the formation of radical species and reactive oxygen species (ROSs) that negatively impact molecules, including proteins. Further oxidative processes at the expense of proteins can be triggered by the product of oxidation, which can consequently alter the storage stability and nutritional and sensorial properties of food. The antioxidant activity of BSH and phenolic fractions can help mitigate this challenge in pet foods when applied in formulations. The peptides in BSH have antimicrobial properties, which can improve product shelf life by protecting against microbial spoilage and reducing pathogenic bacteria in the gut. Hydroxycinnamic acids convey antioxidant effects similar to α-tocopherol and ascorbic acid by scavenging free reactive oxygen and reactive nitrogen species. Ferulic acid possesses antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and food preservative/antimicrobial properties, while p-coumaric acid displays antioxidant and chemo-protectant activities. The presence of these compounds may confer significant health benefits against oxidative damage of cells, inflammation and ensure product safety and shelf stability when applied in pet and farm animal food development. With the application of BSH in animal food, these peptides may help control gut-related diseases such as cryptosporidium, panleukopenia, septicemia, salmonellosis, giardiasis, coccidiosis, acidosis, bovine virus diarrhoea in animals such as cats, dogs and cattle reducing the need for antibiotics. Additionally, the polyphenolic content of BSH and active fractions may exhibit significant antimicrobial activity against pathogens.

The control of hypertension and kidney disease in pets and farm animals may be possible with BSH, particularly due to its demonstrated ACE inhibitory activity linked to the Pro and Ala residues. When BSH is applied in animal food it may help control these metabolic conditions in animals through its glycaemic modulatory activities. The phenolic compounds in BFF may have antioxidant, anti-cancer, anti-atherogenic and anti-inflammatory effects against inflammatory conditions such as inflammatory bowel disease, colitis, gastritis and pancreatitis inanimals. It may also help in the management of chronic diseases such as cardiovascular disease, type-2 diabetes, and obesity.

Dietary fibre has found applications in pet foods and farm animal feed owing to its functional benefit. BSG dietary fibre fractions may equally be applied in the formulation of pet and farm animal feed to deliver dietary fibre benefits not limited to modulation of microbiota, decrease in gastric emptying, blood cholesterol concentrations, gastric transit time, dilution in diet calorie density as well as increase in satiety. AXs, the most prevalent hemicellulose in BSG, are shown to increase bulk viscosity, delay gastric emptying, and maintain blood glucose and insulin homeostasis. BSG insoluble lignin may contribute to systemic antioxidant health benefits by increasing the circulating phenolic pool in pets and farm animals. Raza et al examined the hypocholesterolemic effects of BSG-insoluble lignin in obese mice. The intervention led to a significant reduction in fasting plasma low-density lipoprotein and total cholesterol levels by approximately 35and 20 %.

The improved physicochemical and techno-functional properties of BSH offer significant benefits in animal food formulations. These qualities may improve mixing, texture, palatability, and flavour retention, supporting the development of functional and appealing animal food products. BSH, a cereal by-product, can be used as cereal flour, while its improved thermostability may help control Maillard’s reaction, which accelerates at higher temperatures, leading to flavour and colour changes and potentially decreasing protein digestibility. Additionally, BSH can improve oxidative stability which may ensure the shelf stability of formulated animal foods. The antimicrobial, and antioxidant potential of BBF can be beneficial regarding shelf life when applied in food and feed formulation, as they can combat pro-oxidants and microbes that may facilitate product deterioration.

The choice of raw materials to produce pet food and farm animal feed is thus an essential step for the manufacturing companies as they must provide the necessary nutrients for animal health, development and maintenance. BBF can be leveraged to deliver these benefits while promoting sustainability in the brewing industry. Utilizing BSG by-products in pet food may help the animal food/feed industry avoid competing with human food while reducing the environmental impact.

## 8. ~~Safety and regulatory considerations~~ (Безопасность и регуляторные аспекты)

~~Human and animal food manufacturing regulations~~ (Правила производства пищи для людей и животных) share many similarities, with the key difference **being** (являясь) that ~~pet food~~ (корм для домашних животных) is exempt from ~~allergen controls~~ (контроля аллергенов), as allergens are not **considered** (считаются) a ~~safety risk~~ (риск для безопасности) for animals due to the lack of ~~anaphylactic reactions~~ (анафилактических реакций). ~~Pet and animal food hazards~~ (Опасности кормов для домашних и сельскохозяйственных животных) **include** (включают) mycotoxins in the ingredients (legumes, grains, etc.), ~~residual antibiotics~~ (остаточные антибиотики), pathogens like Salmonella and Listeria monocytogenes, ~~foreign materials~~ (инородные материалы) (e.g., metal, plastic), ~~heavy metals~~ (тяжёлые металлы), ~~environmental contaminants~~ (загрязнители окружающей среды) (e.g., dioxins, PCBs), and ~~chemical risks~~ (химические риски) such as ~~unapproved additives~~ (неодобренные добавки), ~~residual pesticides~~ (остаточные пестициды), and toxins.

Pet food manufacturers particularly need a robust food safety culture (FSC) to meet regulatory requirements and pet owners’ expectations and preferences. The FSC must include supplier assessments, approvals, and certificates of analysis (COAs) of ingredient. A well-designed FSC will helps pet food businesses manage food safety failures, reduce recall costs, prevent process control issues, and aid in creating procedures to avoid hazards that could lead to costly recalls and regulatory violations.

In the United States, pet food is regulated by the FDA. The Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points (HACCP) principles, as outlined in the international CODEX standards, remain the standard for all pet food exporters. The pet food industry is also regulated by the Food Modernization Safety Act (FSMA), which mandates all pet foods to be processed under Hazard Analysis and Risk-Based Preventive Controls for Food for Animals (PCAF regulation) principles as documented in the 21CFR§507. Additionally, as part of FSMA, pet food importers must have a Foreign Supplier Verification Program (FSVP) documented under 21CFR§1, Subpart L. For over 110 years, the Association of American Feed Control Officials (AAFCO) has guided state, federal, and international feed regulators, approving ingredient definitions, label standards, and protocols to ensure the health and safety of people and animals. AAFCO oversees feed production, ensures compliance, and subjects non-compliant products to FDA recalls.

In the European Union, the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) offers scientific advice in the areas that influence food and feed safety. FEDIAF which collaborates with EFSA, and the European Commission represents around 650 companies responsible for producing more than 90 % of European pet food. FEDIAF has established Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) to ensure pet food quality, including selecting reliable suppliers, defining specifications, and testing for contaminants. GMP also covers controlled milling, precise ingredient measurements, microbial testing, foreign material detection, packaging checks, traceability, and nutritional verification. The Nutritional and Analytical Sciences Working Group of FEDIAF which cooperates with a Scientific Advisory Board of 10 leading nutritionists from different European universities developed nutritional guidelines for cats and dogs, which are updated as soon as new scientific data becomes available. FEDIAF has provided self-regulating industry Codes namely the “Guide to Good Practice for the Manufacture of Safe Pet Foods” , the “FEDIAF Code of Good Labelling Practice for Pet Food”, and the “Nutrition Guidelines for Complete and Complementary Pet Food for Cats and Dogs” recognized by EU authorities. In the EU, two laws set out the basic principles for food and feed safety: ‘general food law’ which also applies to feed and ‘feed hygiene’.

Although brewing steps like steeping, kilning, mashing, boiling, fermentation, and clarification significantly reduce beer and BSG pollutant levels keeping residues low and safe for use as animal feed. However, to maximize the potential of BBF for pet and animal food development, stringent measures must be ensured throughout the entire chain of production to marketing to comply with national, regional and international safety guidelines and regulatory mandates.

### 10. ~~Environmental and economic impact~~ (Экологическое и экономическое воздействие)

Applying BBF for functional foods promotes environmental sustainability by diverting significant quantities of BSG from landfills and consequently, reducing the emission of potent greenhouse gases such as CH₄ and CO₂. This will help pet and farm animal food manufacturers to lessen dependence on traditional agricultural inputs, which often require vast land use and intensive resource cultivation. Integrating BBF into pet and farm animal food exemplifies circular bioeconomy principles by transforming by-products into valuable resources. The functional pet food market has experienced significant growth, driven by the increasing willingness of pet owners to invest in their pets’ health and well-being by prioritizing products with added health benefits. BBF can be explored in pet and farm animal food to deliver health benefits, meeting animal farmers’ and pet owners’ expectations. These ingredients can position products in the premium segment, addressing market demand.

In November 2022, the European Council formally approved the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) to standardize sustainability reporting, urging companies to disclose their environmental, social, and governance (ESG) impacts. BBF in pet and animal food align with this directive by promoting a circular economy. By transforming brewing by-products into valuable ingredients, companies can reduce waste, lower environmental impact, and enhance resource efficiency, which are key CSRD goals. This integration supports the CSRD’s focus on responsible sourcing and sustainable production. Additionally, using BBF can improve the ESG performance of food companies, showcasing sustainable practices and innovation in waste management. Companies using BBF must document their sustainability efforts through audits and digital tagging, aligning with the CSRD’s reporting requirements. By doing so, businesses strengthen their sustainability profile, comply with EU regulations, and differentiate themselves in the competitive pet food market.

As a development of corporate Non-Financial Reporting, "Double Materiality" (DM) introduced by the European Commission (EC) necessitates sharing information on i) how a company’s operations affect society and the environment, and ii) how climate-related events affect the company’s performance. Using BBF helps to minimize environmental impact, reduces reliance on virgin resources, and contributes to sustainability goals such as waste reduction and climate change mitigation. Additionally, regulatory pressures, such as those from the CSRD, require companies to demonstrate environmental and social responsibility. Incorporating BBF helps to mitigate risks related to waste management, and environmental compliance. Through the Double Materiality Assessment, companies can understand how using BBF benefits both the environment and their financial performance, positioning them for long-term sustainability and market success.
